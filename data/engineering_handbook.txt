RETAIL TECHNOLOGY GROUP — ENGINEERING HANDBOOK
Version 4.2 | Internal Use Only | Owner: Engineering Operations Council

============================================================
SECTION 1: ENGINEER ONBOARDING
============================================================

1.1 First-Week Checklist
All new engineers complete the "Runway" onboarding track within their
first 10 working days. The Runway track includes:
  - Day 1-2: Laptop provisioning, VPN access, and badge activation via
    the ServiceHub portal. Manager approves access tiers.
  - Day 3-4: Codebase orientation. Every new engineer is assigned an
    Onboarding Buddy from their pod for the first 30 days.
  - Day 5-7: First "starter ticket" — a low-risk, well-scoped change
    merged to a non-critical service. The goal is one merged pull
    request within the first two weeks.
  - Day 8-10: Shadow one full on-call handoff meeting and one
    production deployment.

1.2 Access Policy
Production database read access requires completion of the Data
Handling certification (renewed annually). Write access to production
systems is never granted to engineers in their first 60 days.

1.3 The 30/60/90 Expectations
  - 30 days: merging small changes independently; knows pod's services.
  - 60 days: owns a feature workstream; participates in code review.
  - 90 days: eligible to join the on-call rotation after completing
    the Incident Response workshop and shadowing two on-call shifts.

============================================================
SECTION 2: ON-CALL POLICY
============================================================

2.1 Rotation Structure
Each pod runs a weekly on-call rotation with two roles:
  - PRIMARY: first responder for all pages.
  - SECONDARY: backup; automatically paged if the primary does not
    acknowledge within the escalation window.
Rotations hand off every Tuesday at 10:00 local time in the pod's
on-call handoff meeting.

2.2 Acknowledgement and Escalation Windows
  - Severity 1 (SEV-1, customer-facing outage): the primary must
    acknowledge the page within 5 minutes. If unacknowledged, the
    page escalates to the secondary, then to the Engineering Manager
    at the 15-minute mark.
  - Severity 2 (SEV-2, degraded experience): acknowledge within
    15 minutes.
  - Severity 3 (SEV-3, non-urgent defect): acknowledge within
    4 business hours.

2.3 Incident Roles
Every SEV-1 incident requires three named roles within the first
20 minutes: an Incident Commander (IC), a Communications Lead who
posts updates to the #incident-bridge channel every 30 minutes, and
a Scribe who maintains the incident timeline.

2.4 On-Call Health Rules
  - No engineer may serve more than one primary week per three-week
    period without Engineering Manager sign-off.
  - Engineers paged more than twice between 22:00 and 06:00 receive
    the following morning off ("recovery morning") — this is policy,
    not a courtesy.
  - Post-incident reviews (PIRs) are blameless and must be scheduled
    within 5 business days of any SEV-1 or SEV-2.

============================================================
SECTION 3: DEPLOYMENT POLICY
============================================================

3.1 Standard Deployment Windows
Production deployments are permitted Monday through Thursday,
09:00-16:00 local time. Friday deployments require Director
approval. No deployments on weekends or company holidays except
approved emergency fixes.

3.2 Peak-Season Change Freeze
A change freeze is in effect each year from November 15 through
January 5 covering all customer-facing retail systems (point-of-sale,
checkout, inventory, and order management). During the freeze:
  - Only P0 emergency fixes may ship, with VP Engineering approval.
  - All freeze-exception deployments require a rollback plan tested
    in staging and a named on-call owner watching dashboards for
    60 minutes post-deploy.

3.3 Deployment Requirements (All Environments)
Every production deployment must have:
  1. A green run on the full CI pipeline (unit, integration, and
     contract tests).
  2. Peer review approval from at least one engineer outside the
     authoring pair.
  3. A canary stage: new builds serve 5% of traffic for a minimum of
     30 minutes before full rollout. Error-rate increase above 0.5%
     versus baseline triggers automatic rollback.
  4. A one-line rollback path. If rollback takes more than one
     command or one click, the deployment plan is rejected.

3.4 Feature Flags
All customer-facing changes ship behind feature flags. Flags default
to OFF in production. Stale flags must be removed within 90 days of
full rollout; the flag-debt report is reviewed monthly by each
Engineering Manager.
